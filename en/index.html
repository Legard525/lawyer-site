About

Twenty-five years in Russian courts

I have practiced civil law in Moscow since 2001. Courts change judges, codes gain amendments, but one thing has stayed constant: cases are won by preparation — knowing the file better than anyone else in the courtroom.

Today a large part of my work is for clients who no longer live in Russia yet still have property, family or inheritance matters here. Distance changes the logistics, not the outcome: with a properly issued power of attorney, nearly every civil matter can be brought to a close without your presence.

Services

What I handle for clients abroad

Four areas cover most of what emigrants need in Russia. Each can be handled start to finish under a power of attorney.

Inheritance across borders

Accepting or contesting an inheritance in Russia while living abroad: restoring missed deadlines, notarial procedures, disputes between heirs, registration of title.

Real estate & housing

Remote support for the sale or purchase of property, recognition of ownership, tenant and rent disputes, eviction, protection of your apartment while you are away.

Family matters

Divorce between residents of different countries, division of marital property located in Russia, alimony, disputes concerning children.

Courts & representation

Full representation in Russian courts of all instances under power of attorney — you follow the case from wherever you live.

Also: consumer protection, employment disputes, debt recovery, contract review.

Process

Remote representation, step by step

01

Video consultation

We discuss your situation over a call — in Russian or English. You receive a clear assessment: prospects, timeline, cost.

02

Power of attorney

You sign a power of attorney at the nearest Russian consulate, or before a local notary with an apostille. I prepare the exact wording for your case in advance.

03

I act in Russia

Courts, notaries, registries, negotiations — handled in person in Moscow and, for some cases, in the Moscow Region.

04

You stay informed

Regular written updates by WhatsApp or e-mail. Documents and court acts reach you as scans — with translation when needed.

A power of attorney certified at a Russian consulate is equivalent to a notarized one and needs no apostille. Before your consulate appointment, I draft the precise wording so nothing has to be redone.
